SpaceX caught the **Ship 42** upper stage with the chopsticks on Starbase pad 2 at **21:20 UTC on 14 November**, about 22 hours after launching from the same pad. The ship had deployed 26 Version 3 Starlink satellites, then coasted through **14 orbits** before reentering over the Pacific, crossing Mexico and the south-western United States supersonically and lining itself up for the catch. It is the first time the upper stage of an orbital launch vehicle has returned to Earth and been recovered.

The booster did not make it. **Super Heavy B22** aborted its own catch about 6.5 minutes after liftoff when guidance detected it was not where the catch required, and diverted to an offshore splashdown, where it exploded as remaining propellant burned off. SpaceX called the abort automatic and within the flight envelope's rules. Version 3 of Super Heavy has still not landed, though three earlier boosters were caught.

Spaceflight Now reported both outcomes from the mission. The ship recovery is the step that keeps Starship's full-reuse claim alive; the booster abort means the next flight retries the half that has already worked before.
