In a post published on 22 November, OpenAI chief scientist **Jakub Pachocki** argued that **perplexity**, or cross-entropy loss on a held-out set, should become the metric labs compare models by and anchor shared safety standards. OpenAI disclosed its own numbers for the first time: **GPT-3.5 at about 0.99** log-perplexity, **GPT-4 at about 0.91**, and transformative capabilities in cybersecurity and mathematics appearing below **0.8**.

Pachocki ties the proposal to a specific observation. Once OpenAI moved to a model at roughly **0.76** log-perplexity, it began producing results on long-open mathematics problems, which he reads as evidence that further capability jumps are plausible in pretraining research itself, possibly starting in **2027** as AI takes part in algorithmic work. He expects the next window at **0.68 to 0.70** to bring capabilities nobody is testing for today, alongside further degradation of alignment auditing.

The concrete ask is a set of **common limits**: thresholds based on perplexity and other trackable metrics that frontier developers commit not to cross until monitoring reaches a defined milestone, such as a successor to chain-of-thought monitors. Pachocki notes the metric's limits, since labs train on different datasets and perplexity does not capture every capability. Publishing the numbers also hands competitors a view of where OpenAI stands, a cost he says is outweighed by the need for a shared basis for pacing.
