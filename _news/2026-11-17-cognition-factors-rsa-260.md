A Cognition researcher drove a fleet of Devin agents to build a GPU lattice siever, then used it to factor **RSA-260**, a 260-digit number from the RSA Factoring Challenge, into two 130-digit primes. Work started on 18 August, sieving ran for eight days, and the factors came out on 3 September 2026. The blog reports it as the largest publicly solved challenge number.

The author estimates the run at **4,900 GPU-days**, or about 13.5 GPU-years, and roughly **$400,000** at current market prices. The same siever factors numbers at 10x lower cost than the previous public state of the art.

RSA is not broken. The work speeds up the general number field sieve on GPUs rather than finding a new method, and the blog puts RSA-1024 at 78 times the computation of RSA-260 and RSA-2048 about a billion times harder than RSA-1024.
