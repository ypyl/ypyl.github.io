OpenAI has disbanded its stand-alone robotics team and moved the researchers elsewhere in the company, Fortune reported on 13 November and OpenAI confirmed. Robotics research now sits in the main research organisation reporting to Chief Research Officer **Mark Chen**, while the applied robotics and hardware teams moved under president **Greg Brockman**. A spokesperson described the move as a way to speed up robotics work rather than retreat from it.

The reasoning is that a separate robotics stack is no longer the fastest path. At the start of 2026, OpenAI's researchers assumed progress would come from building on video generation models such as Sora, which pick up the physics of the world implicitly. General frontier models turned out to handle robots directly. In an Nvidia study cited in the report, an agent running **GPT-6 Astra** without any robotics-specific training hit **74.8% success on a simulated manipulation benchmark**, against 51.0% for the best specialised models on the same leaderboard.

OpenAI first built a robotics team in 2024 alongside a partnership with Figure, which ended in February 2025 when Figure said it would build its own robot AI. The company says it is developing its own humanoid, with Sam Altman targeting a first public demonstration in **2027**.
