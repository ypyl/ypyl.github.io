Microsoft used Ignite 2026 on November 18 to launch seven MAI models and a new way to buy them. **MAI Code 2** averages **98%** of **GPT-6.1 Sol's** quality at **40%** of the cost and **96%** of **Claude Opus 5.5's** at **5%**, measured over DeepSWE 1.1, Terminal Bench 2.1, SWE-Atlas and FrontierCode 1.1 on Microsoft's own MSBench harness, and developers preferred it to GPT-6 Sol in **64%** of 258 blind frontend comparisons. The cost figures assume a 50% promotional price, which doubles them at list.

The rest of the batch, with the numbers Microsoft published:

- **MAI Code 2 Flash**, the high-volume variant, matches GPT-6 Luna at **65%** of the cost and Haiku 5.5 at **20%**.
- **MAI Cyber 2 Flash** scores **92.3%** on **CyberGym-E2E** against **91.5%** for GPT-6 Astra at under **2%** of the cost per task, with a 1 million token context. Microsoft dropped **92 of the benchmark's 905 tasks** after finding they drew on the same open source projects and vulnerability reports as its training data, and reports the score on the remaining 813.
- **Microsoft-Decision-1.1** scores **68** on the Artificial Analysis Decision Index v2.1, six points above the first version and ahead of Gemini Decision 1 and GPT-6 Luna, at $0.042 per million tokens.

MAI Image 2.7, MAI Realtime 1 and Dragon Copilot Transcribe fill out the list. Separately, **MAI Licensed Weights** lets a business fine-tune MAI models and run them inside its own Azure environment, available through Frontier Company engagements. Microsoft states plainly that Code 2 is not tuned for fundamental science research or very long-horizon agentic work.
