Australian startup **Apate.ai**, founded in 2024 on research from Macquarie University in Sydney, runs thousands of AI agents that pose as ordinary victims: they answer scam calls, reply to phishing messages, and join investment groups to keep fraudsters talking. The company says the fleet has burned **17 years of scammers' time across 2.5 million conversations**, and the point is the intelligence that comes out of them.

The agents extract details normally learned only after someone has already been robbed: bank accounts, crypto wallets, phone numbers, malicious sites, and the brands a scammer is impersonating. That output goes to Apate's customers, which include **Commonwealth Bank of Australia** and **TPG Telecom**; TPG has diverted nearly **590,000 calls** to the agents, and Commonwealth reports a **76% drop** in publicly reported scam losses.

Apate raised **$8.15 million** in seed funding in August, bringing its total to just under $10 million, and plans a London office plus a larger US presence. The pitch lands in a market the FBI describes as growing: it logged over **22,000 AI-related crime complaints in 2025** with roughly **$893 million** in losses, two thirds of it from investment scams.
