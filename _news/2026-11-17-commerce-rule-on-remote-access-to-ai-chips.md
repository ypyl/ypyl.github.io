The US Bureau of Industry and Security released an interim final rule on 16 November that puts licenses on **remote access** to advanced chips and servers, not just their shipment. Under the rule, a data center in a third country that serves a customer in a restricted country needs export authorization before it runs that hardware, which closes the route Chinese AI companies used after direct sales were banned. BIS says Treasury and the National Security Council directed the rule.

The case behind it is **Megaspeed International**, a Singapore data center operator with facilities in Malaysia. BIS opened a criminal investigation into the company in 2025 over selling remote access to American chips, and Nvidia had placed it on a "no sell" list by June 2025. The same rule adds Indonesia, Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand and Brazil to the list of destinations that need a license for advanced chip exports.

Nvidia had argued against tightening the controls, saying broad restrictions keep American hardware out of world markets, according to The Information.
