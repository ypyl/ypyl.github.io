Anthropic has deposited a 42-page paper, credited to **Claude Fable 5.2**, that settles how a black hole forms in a universe small enough to have no classical geometry. **Hawking and Page** showed in 1983 that a black hole appears in anti-de Sitter space through a first-order phase transition, which in **N=4 super Yang-Mills** on a three-sphere is deconfinement at strong coupling. In **2003** physicists suggested the weak-coupling transition at the Hagedorn temperature could connect continuously to that one, either as a single first-order line or as a pair of continuous transitions with no gravity counterpart. The sign of one number decides it: the quartic coefficient **b** of the Polyakov-loop effective action, which first appears at three loops and had not been computed in the two decades since.

The paper finds **b = −1.48×10⁻³ λ²**, so the transition stays first order, sits slightly below the Hagedorn temperature, and its three saddle points appear, exchange dominance and disappear in the same order as thermal anti-de Sitter space and the small and large black holes. The Hamiltonian computation derives almost every number two independent ways, and it reproduces the two-loop Hagedorn temperature known from integrability.
