Nvidia's board has closed the door on **Jensen Huang's** return. Chairman **Harvey Jones** opened the quarterly earnings call on 17 November by saying the board "unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO". CFO **Colette Kress** continues to run the company while the board searches for a permanent replacement, and Jones said it will not speed the search up at the cost of the right hire.

Huang had posted earlier the same day that employees, investors, partners and customers were asking him to come back, and that he would be "honoured" to serve again if the board judged it in the interests of shareholders and the country. He acknowledged "serious mistakes" in his personal life and said he would cooperate with an independent board investigation. A "Stand with Jensen" petition collected signatures from current and former employees, investors and Elon Musk, and President Trump said on Monday that Huang "learned a big lesson" and that he "would certainly not mind" a return.

The resignation followed Huang's admission of extramarital affairs, including one with a Chinese businesswoman who is an executive at Singapore-based cloud firm **Megaspeed International**, a company US authorities are investigating over allegedly diverting advanced Nvidia chips to Chinese buyers.

Congressional scrutiny continues regardless of the board's decision. Huang has agreed to appear for transcribed interviews before House committees on **1 December**, with Nvidia general counsel **Tim Teter** following on **2 December**, according to the chairmen of three committees.
