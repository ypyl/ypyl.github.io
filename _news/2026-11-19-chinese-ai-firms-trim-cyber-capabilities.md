Access to the most capable Chinese models now comes with less offensive cyber ability. Bloomberg reported on November 16 that recent releases from **Alibaba**, **Tencent** and **Moonshot AI** ship with scaled-down attack capabilities and stronger defensive behaviour, after Beijing proposed an export ban on AI tools that can hit critical infrastructure, government networks or military targets.

The three changes are not the same shape:

- **Tencent** removed cyber functions from **Hy4** before releasing it this month, keeping its open weights.
- **Moonshot** did the same with **Kimi K3.1 Pro**, which still went out with downloadable weights on November 4.
- **Alibaba** published the latest **Qwen4-Max** without weights at all, so the flagship cannot be downloaded and reconfigured the way earlier Qwen releases could, while promising open weights for the smaller Qwen4 models.

The context behind it: China's Ministry of Commerce put the export ban proposal out last month, the National Development and Reform Commission said in early November that it is accelerating safety standards and ethical guidelines, and CrowdStrike researchers reported in October that attackers had used a DeepSeek model against organisations in **14 countries**. Tencent's chief AI scientist Yao Shunyu put the industry's reading bluntly at a Hong Kong event this month: the cyber ability of models, and soon the biological ability, will reach a threshold where harm is possible.

The reported limit is worth keeping: stripping offensive material out of training is a cheap and effective filter, not a guarantee, according to the founder of the AI safety firm Fangcun AI, since no current method removes the risk entirely.
