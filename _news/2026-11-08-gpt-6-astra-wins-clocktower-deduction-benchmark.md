Podcaster Dwarkesh Patel spent **$20,000** on API calls to run a tournament of **11 models** playing **Blood on the Clocktower**, a social deduction game where a minority of evil players know each other while the good majority must reconstruct the truth from nightly hints. To separate the two skills, one model played the entire good camp and another the entire evil camp, then they swapped roles, across **386 rounds**. **GPT-6 Astra** took first place with an Elo of **1859** against **1549** for Claude Opus 5.5, and it also led on detecting lies (**1940** vs **1500**) and on producing them (**1806** vs **1585**).

Individual games showed recognisably human patterns: Astra sacrificed its own ally to the vote to earn trust, while Opus 5.5 took a rival's claim at face value where Astra rechecked the evidence and found the bluff. Across this year's releases, OpenAI is advancing faster at spotting disinformation while both labs improve at deception at a similar pace.

The harness is open at [github.com/dwarkeshsp/clocktower](https://github.com/dwarkeshsp/clocktower), and anyone can play against the models at [play.clocktowerbench.com](https://play.clocktowerbench.com/).
