OpenRouter **off-boarded three inference providers** and removed **125 endpoints** from Zero Data Retention eligibility, the company said on 22 November. The action followed research from **Dragos**, published earlier that week, which found that certain inference providers had data compromised or had shown evidence their data policies were not being upheld. OpenRouter says its own platform was not breached and that the findings concern the third-party providers running the models.

Zero Data Retention eligibility is what lets a company send source code, documents or other confidential material through an API on the promise that nothing is stored after the response. The 125 removals cover endpoints from the flagged providers plus additional ones OpenRouter pulled as a precaution. Affected customers were notified directly, and the company says it will require assurances and validation before restoring ZDR to any of them.

Four providers described what happened in public posts: **Together** reported prompt leakage for requests made in June and July 2026, **Novita** issued a privacy notice about MiniMax requests, **Phala** published a preliminary incident report, and **Atlas Cloud** posted a security commitment. OpenRouter lists those links itself and notes that not every affected provider has written a public account. It is also adapting the Dragos testing method into an ongoing check and verifying network paths for requests forwarded to third parties.
